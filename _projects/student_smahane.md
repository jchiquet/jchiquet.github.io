### Summary

This thesis investigates how gene expression is reprogrammed following allopolyploidization in wheat, the process by which a new species arises from the combination and duplication of two parental genomes. Using transcriptomic data from newly formed and stabilized wheat allopolyploids, it characterizes patterns of non-additive gene expression -- genes whose expression in the hybrid departs from the simple average of the parental levels -- and relates these patterns to genome-wide gene expression additivity previously observed in genetically stable wheat allohexaploids.
